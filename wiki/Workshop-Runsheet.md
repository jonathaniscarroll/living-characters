# Workshop Runsheet

A suggested pacing guide for a ~1 hour Art Camp session using Living Characters.

## Materials needed

- One facilitator laptop with the tool open in a browser
- Felt, clay, or other physical sculpting materials
- A phone or tablet for photogrammetry scanning (optional)
- Projector or large screen for the group to watch

---

## Session structure

### 0:00 – 0:10 — Warm-up: who lives here?

Show the map on screen. Point to the rooms that already exist. Ask participants: *"Who might live in this room? What are they like?"* Take 2–3 suggestions as a group.

### 0:10 – 0:25 — Make your character (physical)

Participants use felt and clay to build a small physical version of their character. Facilitator circulates and asks about the character's name, mood, and one secret.

### 0:25 – 0:35 — Enter the character into the tool

Facilitator inputs each character one at a time:
1. Add a Character — name, mood, assign to a room
2. Take a photo of the physical character and upload it as the character photo
3. Fill in the hello and secret dialogue prompts from what the participant described
4. Save

### 0:35 – 0:45 — Enter the rooms

Open each room on the shared screen. Let the group watch the characters wander. Invite each participant to tap their own character and read their dialogue aloud.

### 0:45 – 0:55 — Add a room together

As a group, decide on one new room. The facilitator adds it via the modal — room name, lede, and an uploaded backdrop photo (taken during the session, e.g. a photo of the building or a painted backdrop).

### 0:55 – 1:00 — Export + close

1. Export Twee — download the story file.
2. GitHub sync — commit the session state.
3. Show the map with all the new pins as a closing image.

---

## Notes

- **Thursday is always beach day** — if running on a Thursday, do the session outdoors and photograph the beach as a backdrop.
- The tool works on mobile browsers — participants can view the map and tap characters on their own phones if they have the URL, though authoring is facilitator-only.
- Keep dialogue prompts short — two or three sentences per passage is plenty for this age group.
